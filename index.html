What Framer does, and what it needs help with

Framer is the right call for this site. It is also not a commerce platform, and the honest version of the build plan says so on the first page rather than in month two.

RequirementIn FramerHow it actually works
Custom type (Domaine, Inter)Native Custom fonts upload directly. Check the Domaine licence covers web use and the traffic band before launch.
Gradients, scroll and motionNative Framer's strength. Budget it: animate transform and opacity, not layout properties, or mobile suffers.
CMS for speakers, talks, FAQsNative Collections plus components. Structure it once, properly, because restructuring later rewrites every page that binds to it.

The one to decide early

Tickets and giving are two different jobs. Tickets need inventory, tiers and a cut-off. Giving needs Gift Aid and recurring. Trying to serve both with one tool is where this kind of build usually goes wrong, and the decision belongs in stage one, not stage three.

The redirect that cannot save you

Your page table says the new Conference page is "similar to the current home". That sentence is the whole migration risk, and it is worth deciding on deliberately rather than discovering it after launch.

What happens

Today your homepage is the conference page. In the new structure the homepage becomes a general introduction and the conference content moves elsewhere. The URL keeps its history but changes subject, and the content that earned that history arrives at an address with none.

2
New pages stay out of search until launch

You asked for this. Noindex while in review, removed deliberately on the day, not left on by accident. It is the single most common launch mistake on a staged rebuild.

3

Old to new, every row justified. Redirect chains collapse to a single hop.

4
Watch Search Console for six weeks after

Rankings move after a migration whatever you do. The difference between a dip and a problem is whether someone is looking.

The design decision underneath

Every block above is driven by a collection, so adding a speaker is filling a form, not laying out a page. Where a layout would break with eight speakers instead of four, the component handles it, and the team never has to know that was a risk.

1
A written editing guide, built around their jobs

Not a tour of Framer. Six short entries: add a speaker, add a talk, change the ELDC status, update a date, add an FAQ, swap a hero image.

2
A short screen recording for each

Two or three minutes each. People watch these; they do not read manuals.

3
One live session with whoever will actually edit

They do it while I watch, rather than watching me do it. Whatever they find confusing gets fixed in the structure, not in the guide.

4
Everything in your name from day one

Framer workspace, domain, Stripe or ticketing, Beacon connection. You own the accounts. I am a collaborator who can be removed without anything breaking.
